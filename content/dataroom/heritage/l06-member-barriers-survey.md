# Member Barriers Survey, 2026

**Source:** Member Insights team, with Lantern Research Group (survey vendor)
**Period:** Fielded April 6 to May 22, 2026
**Owner:** Priya Desai, Director, Member Insights
**Distribution:** Care Management, Pharmacy, Stars Program Office

## Method

- Stratified random sample of members aged 65 and over, plus those under 65 who qualify through disability, drawn from the August 2025 enrollment file
- Mixed mode: a mail questionnaire with phone follow-up, offered in English and Spanish
- 9,600 sampled, 2,410 completed (25.1% response). By book, completion was 1,180 core (29.5%), 760 Lakeshore (19.0%) and 470 Pinecrest (27.4%)
- Weighted to the book's age, sex and dual-status mix. The margin of error at 95% confidence is plus or minus 3.6 points for Lakeshore
- 1,214 sampled addresses (12.6%) returned no response and no return mail, so their status is unknown

## Verbatims

1. "The bus to the pharmacy takes two transfers. I let the pills run out until my daughter can drive me." (Lakeshore, age 81)
2. "We moved in with my sister last winter. I don't think the plan knows where I live." (Lakeshore, dual eligible)
3. "A pharmacist called once and helped me switch to 90-day mail order. That was the best thing the plan ever did." (Heritage core, age 77)
4. "I get letters about tests I already had." (Pinecrest, age 72)
5. "My phone was shut off for two months. Nobody could reach me and I couldn't reach them." (Lakeshore, age 69)
6. "The clinic is fine. Getting there is the problem." (Lakeshore, age 84)

## Analyst notes

- Lakeshore's two biggest barriers, transport and housing instability, are social barriers that clinical outreach alone will not fix.
- The lower Lakeshore response rate means its results lean toward members the plan can still reach by mail.
