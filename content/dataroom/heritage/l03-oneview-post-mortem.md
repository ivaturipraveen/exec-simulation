# Prior Initiative Post-Mortem: "OneView" Program

**From:** Fiona Brennan, Director, Internal Audit, with the Enterprise Program Management Office (PMO)
**To:** Margaret Ellison, Chief Executive Officer; Board Audit and Quality Committees
**Date:** November 18, 2024 (recirculated September 2, 2026 for transformation planning)
**Classification:** Internal, confidential

## Summary

OneView launched in January 2023 to build a single enterprise view of the member across Heritage core, Lakeshore and Pinecrest. The board cancelled it in August 2024, after 18 months and $9.0M spent ($7.4M external and $1.6M internal labor). It produced no measurable change in any Star Ratings measure. Its scope, its sequencing and the way it reported value are why this envelope exists in its current form.

## What was promised

| Commitment (2023 charter) | Target date | Status at cancellation |
|---|---|---|
| Cloud data lake with all 14 source systems | Q4 2023 | 5 of 14 sources landed |
| Enterprise member master with one golden ID | Q1 2024 | Design approved; not built |
| Single CRM replacing 3 CRMs | Q2 2024 | Vendor selection only |
| Adherence and gap models on unified data | Q3 2024 | Not started |
| +1.0 point on Part D adherence | 2025 rating | No movement |

## Spend profile

| Category | $M |
|---|---|
| Stratavant Consulting (integrator) | 4.6 |

## What went wrong

1. **Everything came first.** The program had to finish the lake, the member master and the CRM before any business user saw value. Nothing reached a care manager, pharmacist or contact-center agent.
2. **Capacity collision.** OneView needed the same 40 integration engineers as regulatory work, including CMS transaction format changes. Change control (a 6-week cycle) put it behind mandatory work on 11 of 14 release trains.
3. **Ownership.** The program sat in IT. The charter named a business owner for each data domain, but those roles were never staffed for the acquired books. When the sponsor (the former Chief Information Officer) left in May 2024, no business leader stepped in.
4. **Measurement.** The steering committee tracked milestones and spend, not leading indicators. After 18 months it could not name a single member-level outcome that had changed.
5. **Vendor model.** Stratavant was paid on time and materials with no value milestones. The termination settlement cost $0.4M.

## What survived

- The partial cloud lake, which holds 5 sources and is used by Analytics, not by the HEDIS engine
- A draft member-matching rule set for Heritage core, which was never extended to Lakeshore
- The data-domain charter, which lapsed

## Board resolution (August 27, 2024, excerpt)

> "Future transformation spending will be released in tranches tied to visible quarterly evidence. The board will not approve another multi-year foundation program without interim measure-level results."

## Lessons recorded

- Fund a foundation only when it is tied to a named use case and a measure.
- Name business owners before engineering starts.
- Report leading indicators every quarter, not just milestones.
- Plan around the claims platform migration, not on top of it.
